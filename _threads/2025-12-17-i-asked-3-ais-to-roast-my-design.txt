---TWEET---
I designed an AI multi-agent system.

Then I asked Gemini, GPT 5.2, and Grok to tear it apart.

They found bugs I never imagined. And they disagreed in fascinating ways. 🧵👇
---TWEET---
The experiment:
• Same spec
• Same prompt: "Be my Devil's Advocate"
• Three different AI models

What I learned: different models have completely different "review personalities"
---TWEET---
Gemini was like a systems architect:
"You'll stare at a spinner for 4 minutes. Users will abandon it."

Focus: workflow, UX, operational reality
---TWEET---
GPT 5.2 was a ruthless auditor:
"This WILL break. That regex injection defense is security theater."

Focus: vulnerabilities, edge cases, enforcement
---TWEET---
Grok was... Grok:
"What if one of your agents is compromised? Who watches the watchers?"

Focus: meta-risks, novel angles, things nobody else asked
---TWEET---
The gold was in their DISAGREEMENTS.

Same feature:
• Gemini: "Latency problem"
• GPT: "Security problem"
• Grok: "Meta-trust problem"

All three were right. The fix had to address all of them.
---TWEET---
GPT's killer line:

"Remove 'find something even if minor.' That one line is going to poison your signal-to-noise ratio faster than anything else."

I had literally trained my AI to generate busywork. 🤦
---TWEET---
The result: spec went from 12 pages → 41 pages.

Not bloat—fixes:
• Evidence validators
• Goal validation
• LLM memory guardrail
• User Proxy agent
• Threat Modeler
• Async job mode
---TWEET---
The meta-lesson:

I was building a system to make AI agents argue productively.

To design it, I made AI agents argue productively.

The spec IS the proof of concept.
---TWEET---
Before you ship your next AI system, try this prompt:

"Be critical. Be adversarial. Be my Devil's Advocate. I want friction, not agreement."
