Your AI coding assistant has a dirty secret:

It forgets EVERYTHING between sessions. 🧠❌

That brilliant convo where it learned your coding style? Gone when you close the chat.

I checked my Memory MCP and found 225 "memories"—90% were garbage.

Here's what I learned 🧵👇
---TWEET---
The problem: Context windows are NOT memory.

What feels like "remembering" is just conversation history fed back each time.

Your context window is:
• Finite (200K tokens fills fast with code)
• Session-bound (dies when you close chat)
• Expensive (every token costs $$$)
---TWEET---
Standard solutions all fail:

❌ Sliding window → drops oldest messages, loses critical decisions made early

❌ Summarization → lossy. JetBrains found agents ran 15% LONGER without solving more

❌ RAG → great for docs, terrible for YOUR personal preferences
---TWEET---
Here's what I tried:

Expose memory as tools. Let the AI decide when to save/retrieve.

```python
@tool
def save_to_memory(entity, fact):
    memory.add(entity, fact)
```

Sounds smart, right?
---TWEET---
Result: GARBAGE DATA 🗑️

• Random file names saved as "entities"
• Duplicate facts everywhere
• Generic observations nobody needs
• Missing the actually important stuff

Why? The AI has cognitive overhead managing YOUR problem AND its memory.
---TWEET---
The fix: Don't make the AI manage its own memory.

Do it AUTOMATICALLY:

1️⃣ Dual-model: Cheap local LLM for memory, expensive model for conversation

2️⃣ Hooks: Memory retrieval on EVERY message, not "when AI decides"

3️⃣ Schema: Define what's worth remembering
---TWEET---
The architecture:

Your message
    ↓
[Local LLM: extract entities, search memory, inject context]
    ↓
[Claude: respond with enriched context]
    ↓
[Local LLM: extract new facts, store async]

<700ms overhead for persistent memory 🚀
