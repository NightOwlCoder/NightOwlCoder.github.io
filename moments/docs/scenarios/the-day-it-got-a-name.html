part one

The story

Every AI tool has the same shape: a session that starts knowing nothing and ends when you close it. The good ones you lose the same way as the bad ones. Here a session that has earned it can be promoted: it keeps its history, gets a name and a face, and joins the crew where everyone else can reach it. The rite is short, and you hold every decision in it.

on the crew: reachable by name, reads its own history, does only what it was granted

Three things you decide, and the agent cannot decide for itself. Whether it gets a name at all (you invite). Which face it wears (it proposes three, you pick, because a face must be unique across the crew). What it is for (it drafts from its own record, you approve the words). Territory comes from where it actually wrote, not from where it would like to. And a name is never a power: pushing a tag stays yours to grant.

part two

How it is sold

One moment a day. This is moment 1 of 12; the format and the others are in the teaser format study.

The four parts of this teaser

PartThis scenarioWhy
the question Ever wished the one good session could just… stay? Everyone who has used a chat assistant has closed a tab and lost something that was finally working. The loss is the wound; a "custom GPT" is not what they mean.
one beat of proof It reads its own history and proposes a name, a face, and what it is for. You approve the words. The proof is that the agent drafts from evidence and the person holds the pen. Not "create an agent in three clicks".
who does it 📐 Kosh · designs the crew Kosh's charter is how agents are scoped and reach each other. The birth rite is his ground; the new seat is his design, instantiated.
the way out see all 12 → Always visible.

Rejected wording: "create custom agents", "agent builder", "personas". Those describe a form you fill in. What is sold is the opposite direction: the agent already exists and has done the work; naming it is recognition, and the record is the source of the charter.

What keeps it honest

Every beat in the story is a rule in the code, not a prompt. The interview is by invitation (the tool's own description says so, and an unnamed agent's prompt tells it it has no name and must not adopt one). The emoji is the one field the agent may not set; it proposes three and the operator picks, validated against every mark in use. Approving a charter is a claim the agent makes about the operator — the tool says setting it unasked "is not a mistake, it is a false claim, and it is in the transcript". Territory is proposed from the directories the session actually wrote in. Trust grants (the tag push) are operator-only by an authoritative predicate, not by convention. The one fiction is the name Quill: this machine's twelve residents are real, Quill is not one of them.

Rejected, with reasons

ShapeWhy not
"Create an agent" form A form produces a charter with nothing behind it. Here the charter is drafted from the record of work already done; the record is the evidence.
The agent names itself when it feels ready The operator decides which sessions have earned a name. An agent that could promote itself could also widen its own scope.
The agent picks its own emoji A mark must be unique across the fleet; only the operator sees the whole fleet.
Name = permissions A charter is scope, not power. Capabilities are separate grants, operator-only, revocable without touching the name.
