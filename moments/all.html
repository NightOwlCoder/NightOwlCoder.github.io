All the moments

One a day, in this order, then it starts again. Each card opens the scenario behind it: the story, how it is sold, how it is built.

moment 1 Ever wished the one good session could just... stay? Give it a name. It does. A session that's done real work can be invited to become someone: it reads its own history and proposes a name, a face, and what it's for. You approve the words. From then on it's on the crew, reachable by name, with only the folders it actually worked in. 📐 Kosh · designs the crew moment 2 Ever set up a "daily" AI task and watched it die by Thursday? The schedule here is a message to someone with a name. Tell an agent to do something every weekday at six. The clock belongs to the engine, not a browser tab: it survives restarts, follows the agent if it moves, and when it can't fire it tells you why instead of going quiet. ⚙️ Salgado · delivery and resume moment 3 Ever told an agent "be careful" and watched it delete something anyway? Ours works alone for an hour, reads and tests and edits, and asks you exactly once, on your phone, the moment it wants to delete something. One tap. It learns the answer and never asks that one again. 🍅 Marco · the authority a gate answers to moment 4 Explained the same thing to your AI three times this month? Say it once. Six weeks later, in a different conversation on a different project, it says: "you decided against that in March, here's why, and here's the session where you did." You can check it. It links. 🗿 Draal · memory and context moment 5 Two agents editing the same file, and the second one undid the first? Ours never share a working copy. Each gets its own, no two may touch the same file at once, and nothing lands until the tests pass on the merged result. If they fail, the merge is undone before you see it. 🍅 Marco · merges and builds moment 6 Want your agent to tell you it's live instead of asking you to review a pull request? You asked for a bug to be fixed. Fourteen minutes later: "it's fixed and live in your copy." No diff, no checklist. The thing you asked for, working. 🌶️ Pepper · chief of staff · one door moment 7 Ever come back to find your agent waiting for a Yes since 10am? The Yes can come from your pocket. Step away and the question follows you. Your phone gets the exact command, why it was stopped, and four buttons: once, no, teach this project, teach everywhere. The rest of the work never stops. 🌶️ Pepper · chief of staff moment 8 Is it stuck, or just slow? Stop refreshing to find out. Every agent here is watched by a clock that isn't an agent. Three minutes of silence with a turn open is a question. Five more and the turn is ended for it, the finished work is kept, and someone is told. Nothing waits for you to notice. ⭐ Polaris · fleet health, measured moment 9 Ever merged AI code because "the tests pass," and the test tested nothing? Here that's a P1. Every piece of work names what would prove it before the code exists: a test, an output, a screenshot. A second agent whose only job is to disbelieve checks each one. A test that would pass against the old code is a finding, not a pass. You review the PR, never the steps. 🍅 Marco · merges and builds moment 10 Closed the laptop and lost the thread? Your agent was never in the laptop. It's a process on the machine you keep on. The desk PC, the box in the closet, a small server. Your laptop, your phone and your terminal are windows onto it. Shut one, open another: same conversation, one turn further, with what happened while you were gone. ⚙️ Salgado · the machinery that carries messages moment 11 How many questions did your agents ask each other today, so they wouldn't have to ask you? Here you can look it up. A question climbs one level at a time: the agent doing the work asks its lead, the lead asks the one who runs the project, that one asks the chief of staff. You're the top of a pyramid that absorbs everything it can. The only questions you hear are the ones that are actually yours. 🌶️ Pepper · chief of staff moment 12 designed, not built What if cloning a repo also cloned the person who knows it? A project can carry its own agent. A seat lives in the repo next to the code: what it's for, where it may write, nothing else. Clone the project and the seat joins your crew, reachable through your chief of staff by the project's name. Birth a better one, send a pull request, and everyone who clones after you gets it too. 📐 Kosh · designs the crew
